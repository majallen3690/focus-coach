- **Shrinking the image hides small phones.** The whole picture is squeezed to 224 x 224 pixels, so a small phone in a wide scene is only a few pixels. In the bottom row of the grid the detector clearly sees the phone and MobileNet gives it a very low probability. The detector works at 640 pixels and can still find it. This is the main reason B-rules is right far more often ({{rules_only_right}} images against {{mn_only_right}}).
- **MobileNet judges the whole scene, the rules judge one relationship.** The cases MobileNet wins (top row) are pictures where the detector finds a head and something phone-like in a misleading place, such as an extreme close-up face or a phone at the side of a seated person. The rules follow the geometry and call it "using"; MobileNet looks at everything and says "not using", which matches the label.
- **MobileNet learned from far less.** It saw {{mn_train}} images with one yes/no label each, while the detector learned from the full training set with a box around every head and phone. The curves show the gap starting to open (final training accuracy {{mn_final_train_acc}}, validation {{mn_final_val_acc}}) and validation was still improving when the epoch limit stopped it, so it could gain a little with more data or epochs, but it is unlikely to close a gap of {{mn_gap_points}} accuracy points ({{c_mobilenet_acc}} against {{b_rules_acc}}).
