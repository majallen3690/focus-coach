- **Enhancement helps only in a narrow band.** On the "dim" copies (median brightness {{bright_dim_median}} of 255) CLAHE with a light blur raised phone mAP50 from {{ll_dim_none_phone}} to {{ll_dim_clahe_smooth_phone}} and head mAP50 from {{ll_dim_none_head}} to {{ll_dim_clahe_smooth_head}}. Plain CLAHE ({{ll_dim_clahe_phone}}) and global histogram equalization ({{ll_dim_global_he_phone}}) made phone detection worse, because they also boost the noise, and the detector reads noise as texture.
- **The "dark" copies are too dark for any method.** At a median brightness of {{bright_dark_median}} (out of 255) the picture information is gone, and phone mAP50 is {{ll_dark_none_phone}} without enhancement and {{ll_dark_clahe_smooth_phone}} with the best method. Enhancement cannot bring back detail that is not there. A real app should tell the user the room is too dark instead of pretending to detect.
- **Real dark photos and normal photos do not need it.** The darkest real test photos (brightness up to {{bright_real_dark_max}}) scored {{ll_real_dark_none_phone}} with no enhancement and slightly less with any enhancement. On normal images the same enhancement changes phone mAP50 from {{ll_original_none_phone}} to {{ll_original_clahe_smooth_phone}} (CLAHE + blur). So enhancement is only worth turning on for genuinely dim frames, which is why the app setting is a brightness threshold (below {{app_threshold}}), not "always on".
- **Usage accuracy follows detection.** With normal lighting B-rules scores {{ll_usage_orig}} accuracy with or without enhancement. At the dark level it predicts "not using" for everything ({{ll_usage_dark}} accuracy, which is just the share of "not using" images), with or without enhancement.
- **Limits of this test.** The darkening is a recipe (scale, gamma, noise), not a real dim room, and the CLAHE settings and method were chosen on the same recipe applied to val images. The real-dark result is the check that does not depend on the recipe, and it says enhancement is not needed there.
